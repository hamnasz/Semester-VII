FYP Introduction

What is DocBot AI?

DocBot AI
Proposal Defense

DocBot AI is proposed as a clinical information-support prototype. It is designed around two capabilities that are proposed to work together rather than as separate tools.

1. Evidence-Grounded Clinical QA

Answers a clinical question by first retrieving relevant material from a medical knowledge base, then generating a response constrained to that retrieved evidence — with the supporting sources shown alongside the answer.

2. Longitudinal Patient Monitoring

Organizes a patient's symptoms and laboratory results into a chronological timeline, analyzes it for meaningful trends, and generates a review-oriented alert when predefined criteria are met.

A review-support prototype — not an autonomous diagnosis system.

Problem Statement

DocBot AI
Proposal Defense

Scattered patient information

Lab reports, clinical notes, symptom records and historical documents live in separate places. Spotting a gradual change means manually comparing values across records — slow, and easy to get wrong.

Unverifiable AI answers

General-purpose conversational AI can answer medical questions, but is not required to ground those answers in any checkable source — risking unsupported or hallucinated claims.

  • Even capable LLM-based clinical QA systems can produce responses that are difficult to verify, including hallucinated references (Low et al., 2025).
  • Retrieval-based grounding is one of the more consistently useful correctives to this problem (Basu & Huynh, 2026) — but published RAG-based medical QA systems ground answers against a general knowledge base, not a specific patient's own history.

Motivation

Motivation & Aim

DocBot AI
Proposal Defense
  • Evidence-grounded medical QA and longitudinal patient monitoring have each been separately validated in published research — but not combined.
  • The closest existing system, EHR-RAG, was not designed to include general medical-knowledge QA or trend-based alerting (Cao et al., 2026).
  • Alert-fatigue research shows that alerts generated without evidence context tend to be ignored or unhelpful (Kwan et al., 2020; Singh et al., 2010) — motivating a system where a flagged trend can be reviewed alongside the evidence that explains it.

Aim

To develop and evaluate DocBot AI, a clinical information-support prototype that combines Retrieval-Augmented Generation and Large Language Models to answer clinical questions with verifiable evidence, while organizing a patient's symptom and laboratory history over time to identify trends worth flagging for professional review.
