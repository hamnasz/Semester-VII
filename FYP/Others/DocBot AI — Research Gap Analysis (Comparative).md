# Research-Gap Analysis for DocBot AI

## A Comparative Analysis of Existing Medical LLM/RAG, Clinical QA, Longitudinal Monitoring, and Decision-Support Systems

---

## 1. Comparative Literature Analysis

### 1.1 What Existing Medical LLM/RAG Systems Primarily Focus On

Existing medical LLM and RAG systems primarily focus on answering medical questions using curated knowledge sources — clinical guidelines, PubMed abstracts, regulatory documents — rather than patient-specific longitudinal data. The dominant thread in this literature is benchmark-driven evaluation: systems are tested on standardized question sets (MedQA, PubMedQA, MedMCQA) to measure factual accuracy, reasoning quality, and safety.

Med-PaLM and Med-PaLM 2 (Singhal et al., 2023; 2025) achieved near-expert performance on multi-choice medical licensing exams (67.6% and 86.5% on MedQA, respectively), but the authors themselves identify grounding responses in authoritative sources and real-world clinical workflow integration as needed research directions ([Nature, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06291-2); [Nature Medicine, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03423-7)). Almanac (Zakka et al., 2024) improved factuality and safety over general-purpose LLMs by retrieving from curated medical resources, but did not incorporate patient-specific longitudinal data, lab trend monitoring, or clinical alerts ([NEJM AI, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2300068)). RAG² (Sohn et al., 2025) improved retrieval quality through rationale-guided queries but similarly operated without patient-specific data ([arXiv:2411.00300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00300)). Zhang et al. (2026) introduced risk classification and evidence grounding to medical QA, but again without longitudinal patient information or monitoring ([JMIR Formative Research, 2026](https://doi.org/10.2196/84932)).

The systematic review by Basu & Huynh (2026) confirmed that RAG is among the most frequently evaluated hallucination mitigation strategies in healthcare AI, and explicitly called for integration of mitigation strategies into clinical workflows ([BMC Health Services Research, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-026-14851-1)). Nanua et al. (2025) demonstrated RAG for clinical laboratory regulatory QA with source display, but was limited to regulatory questions without patient data ([J Pathol Inform, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpi.2025.100520)).

**Summary:** Medical LLM/RAG systems primarily focus on knowledge-grounded question answering against benchmarks or curated corpora. They generally do not integrate patient-specific longitudinal records, laboratory trend detection, or clinical review alerts.

### 1.2 What Existing Clinical QA Systems Focus On

Clinical QA systems, as distinct from general medical LLM systems, focus on building datasets and evaluation frameworks for biomedical question answering. BioASQ (Tsatsaronis et al., 2015) established a standardized benchmark for biomedical semantic indexing and QA, creating the first large-scale evaluation framework for biomedical QA systems ([BMC Bioinformatics, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-015-0564-6)). PubMedQA (Jin et al., 2019) introduced a dataset requiring reasoning over biomedical research abstracts, achieving 68.1% accuracy versus 78.0% human performance, and noted "much room for improvement" ([arXiv:1909.06146](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06146)).

Low et al. (2025) evaluated RAG and agentic LLM systems for real-world clinical questions in deployed settings, finding that no single LLM-based system was sufficient for all clinical question types and that general-purpose LLMs may hallucinate references and treatment guidelines ([Digital Health, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/20552076251348850)). This finding is significant: it suggests that even state-of-the-art clinical QA systems remain fragmented and lack integration with patient-specific monitoring.

**Summary:** Clinical QA systems focus on benchmark construction and evaluation. They validate the feasibility of LLM-based medical question answering but do not connect QA to longitudinal patient monitoring, trend detection, or clinical review alerts.

### 1.3 What Existing Longitudinal Monitoring Systems Focus On

Longitudinal monitoring systems focus on temporal EHR modeling, patient timeline visualization, and structured EHR reasoning. Xie et al. (2022) systematically reviewed deep learning approaches for temporal EHR data, identifying challenges including irregular sampling, missing data, and temporal heterogeneity, but did not propose an integrated QA or monitoring system ([Journal of Biomedical Informatics, 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1532046421003099)).

EHR-RAG (Cao et al., 2026) is the closest identified system to combining RAG with longitudinal EHR data. It proposed type-aware retrieval, multi-path evidence aggregation, and numerical-temporal reasoning over structured EHRs. However, the authors stated that "to the authors' knowledge, no existing RAG framework was explicitly designed to handle the longitudinal, multi-event nature of long-horizon structured EHRs" — and EHR-RAG itself does not include medical knowledge base QA, symptom/lab trend monitoring with predefined criteria, or clinical review alert generation ([arXiv:2601.21340](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21340)).

Almanac Copilot (Zakka et al., 2024) combined LLM-based EHR navigation with retrieval for information retrieval and order placement, but focused on EHR-specific tasks and did not integrate medical knowledge QA, trend detection, or clinical review alerts ([arXiv:2405.07896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07896)).

In the domain of patient timeline visualization, LifeLines (Plaisant et al., 1996/1998) pioneered graphical patient history timelines ([ACM CHI, 1996](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/257089.257391)). Health Timeline (Ledesma et al., 2019) demonstrated that time-based visualization produced higher mean insight value (1.7 vs. 1.26) compared to textual representation ([BMC Med Inform Decis Mak, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-019-0885-x)). HeaRT (Yada & Aramaki, 2023) generated timelines from unstructured EHR text using NLP ([arXiv:2306.14379](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.14379)). HistoriView (Park et al., 2024) addressed scalability problems in timeline visualization ([Applied Clinical Informatics, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2269-0995)). PatientExploreR (Glicksberg et al., 2019) provided interactive EHR exploration in the OMOP common data model ([Bioinformatics, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btz409)).

**Summary:** Longitudinal monitoring systems focus on temporal EHR modeling and visualization. While timeline visualization is a mature field, these systems generally lack AI-powered trend detection, evidence-grounded QA, and clinical alert generation. EHR-RAG is the closest to integrating RAG with longitudinal data but does not include medical knowledge QA or clinical review alerts.

### 1.4 What Existing Clinical Decision-Support Systems Focus On

Clinical decision-support systems (CDSS) focus on prompting evidence-based care, alerting clinicians to abnormal results, and improving adherence to clinical guidelines. The meta-analysis by Kwan et al. (2020) of 122 controlled trials found that CDSS increased the proportion of patients receiving desired care by 5.8% (95% CI 4.0–7.6%), with substantial heterogeneity (I²=76%). The authors stated that "the circumstances under which clinical decision support systems produce worthwhile improvements in care remain unidentified" and that "new ways of designing clinical decision support systems are needed to reliably produce larger improvements in care while balancing alert fatigue" ([BMJ, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m3216)).

The systematic review by Ray et al. (2026) found that alert fatigue measurement methods are not clearly or consistently defined, with only 1 of 22 reviews reporting an operational definition of alert fatigue ([JAMIA, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag064)). Singh et al. (2010) demonstrated that safety concerns related to timely patient follow-up remain despite automated notification of abnormal laboratory results, with 10.2% of alerts unacknowledged and 6.8% lacking timely follow-up ([Am J Med, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjmed.2009.07.027)).

In lab trend detection specifically, Morid et al. (2020) demonstrated structural temporal pattern detection for predicting acute kidney injury in ICU settings, stating that "no previous study had leveraged structural temporal patterns (trends) for adverse-event prediction in critical care" ([JMIR Medical Informatics, 2020](https://doi.org/10.2196/14272)).

**Summary:** CDSS research focuses on measuring effectiveness, managing alert fatigue, and improving follow-up of abnormal results. These studies highlight that simple threshold-based alerts are insufficient and that alert design must account for alert fatigue — but they do not integrate evidence-grounded QA, longitudinal patient context, or RAG-based knowledge retrieval.

### 1.5 Which Components Are Commonly Integrated

The reviewed literature reveals several common integration patterns:

1. **RAG + Medical QA:** Almanac ([Zakka et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2300068)), RAG² ([Sohn et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00300)), and Zhang et al. ([2026](https://doi.org/10.2196/84932)) all integrate retrieval-augmented generation with medical question answering. This pairing has been evaluated in several reviewed systems and improves factuality, completeness, and safety over non-grounded LLMs.

2. **LLM + Medical Knowledge:** Med-PaLM and Med-PaLM 2 ([Singhal et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06291-2); [2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03423-7)) integrate large language models with medical knowledge for expert-level QA. BioBERT ([Lee et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btz682)) and SapBERT ([Liu et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11784)) provide domain-specific embeddings that support biomedical NLP tasks including retrieval.

3. **RAG + Longitudinal EHR:** EHR-RAG ([Cao et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21340)) and Almanac Copilot ([Zakka et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07896)) integrate retrieval-augmented generation with longitudinal EHR data. This pairing is emerging but focuses on structured EHR reasoning rather than medical knowledge QA or trend monitoring.

4. **Timeline Visualization + Longitudinal Data:** LifeLines ([Plaisant et al., 1996](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/257089.257391)), Health Timeline ([Ledesma et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-019-0885-x)), HistoriView ([Park et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2269-0995)), and PatientExploreR ([Glicksberg et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btz409)) integrate timeline visualization with patient data. This pairing is well-established in the visualization literature but lacks AI-powered analysis.

5. **Lab Alerts + CDSS:** Singh et al. ([2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjmed.2009.07.027)) and Kwan et al. ([2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m3216)) examine lab result alerting within CDSS frameworks. This pairing is well-studied but focuses on threshold-based alerts without trend detection or QA integration.

### 1.6 Which Components Are Usually Treated Separately

The reviewed literature consistently treats the following components as separate research threads:

1. **Evidence-grounded QA is separated from longitudinal patient monitoring.** Medical QA systems ([Med-PaLM, Singhal et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06291-2); [Almanac, Zakka et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2300068); [RAG², Sohn et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00300); [Zhang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.2196/84932)) operate on knowledge bases or benchmarks, not on patient-specific longitudinal records. Longitudinal systems ([EHR-RAG, Cao et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21340); timeline visualizations) focus on patient data without integrating medical knowledge QA.

2. **Trend detection is separated from clinical QA and alerting.** [Morid et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.2196/14272) demonstrated temporal pattern detection for ICU AKI prediction but did not connect trend findings to evidence-grounded QA or clinical review alerts. [Singh et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjmed.2009.07.027) examined lab result alerting but used simple thresholds without trend analysis.

3. **Timeline visualization is separated from AI-powered analysis.** Timeline systems ([LifeLines, Plaisant et al., 1996](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/257089.257391); [Health Timeline, Ledesma et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-019-0885-x); [HeaRT, Yada & Aramaki, 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.14379); [HistoriView, Park et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2269-0995); [PatientExploreR, Glicksberg et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btz409)) provide visual representations but do not incorporate LLM-based reasoning, evidence grounding, or automated trend detection.

4. **Clinical alerts are separated from evidence-grounded context.** CDSS research ([Kwan et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m3216); [Ray et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag064); [Singh et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjmed.2009.07.027)) focuses on alert effectiveness and fatigue but does not integrate alerts with RAG-based evidence retrieval or medical knowledge QA.

5. **Source display and evidence provenance are separated from patient monitoring.** While [Almanac (Zakka et al., 2024)](https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2300068) and [Nanua et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpi.2025.100520) demonstrated source display for medical QA, these systems do not connect evidence provenance to patient-specific monitoring or clinical review alerts.

### 1.7 Limitations That Repeatedly Appear in Previous Work

Several limitations recur across the reviewed literature:

1. **Lack of patient-specific data integration.** Medical QA systems ([Med-PaLM, Singhal et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06291-2); [Almanac, Zakka et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2300068); [RAG², Sohn et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00300); [Zhang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.2196/84932)) operate on general knowledge bases without patient-specific longitudinal records. This is identified as a limitation by Med-PaLM authors themselves, who note that grounding in patient-specific data remains a needed research direction ([Nature Medicine, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03423-7)).

2. **Absence of longitudinal trend detection in QA systems.** No reviewed QA system incorporates automated trend detection over laboratory values or symptoms. [Morid et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.2196/14272) demonstrated trend detection but only for ICU AKI prediction, not integrated with QA.

3. **Alert fatigue and insufficient alert design.** [Kwan et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m3216) found that only 14% of CDSS trials considered alert fatigue. [Ray et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag064) found that alert fatigue measurement is inconsistent. [Singh et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjmed.2009.07.027) showed that automated lab alerts alone do not guarantee timely follow-up.

4. **Lack of clinical workflow integration.** [Basu & Huynh (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-026-14851-1) explicitly called for integration of hallucination mitigation strategies into clinical workflows. [Med-PaLM/Med-PaLM 2 authors](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03423-7) identified real-world clinical workflow integration as unstudied.

5. **Fragmentation of capabilities.** [Low et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1177/20552076251348850) found that no single LLM-based system is sufficient for all clinical question types, suggesting that integration across capabilities is needed.

6. **Absence of evidence provenance in monitoring systems.** Longitudinal monitoring and CDSS systems do not typically display evidence sources alongside alerts or trends, limiting clinician trust and verifiability.

7. **Limited real-world deployment.** [Basu & Huynh (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-026-14851-1) identified insufficient real-world deployment studies as a gap in healthcare AI research.

### 1.8 What Specific Gap Can Reasonably Be Addressed by DocBot AI

Based on the comparative analysis, the literature indicates an opportunity to integrate capabilities that have been evaluated individually but not combined into a single platform. Specifically:

- Evidence-grounded clinical QA (evaluated by [Almanac, Zakka et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2300068); [RAG², Sohn et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00300); [Zhang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.2196/84932))
- Medical knowledge retrieval via embeddings and vector search (supported by [BioBERT, Lee et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btz682); [SapBERT, Liu et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11784); [RAG, Lewis et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401))
- Longitudinal patient timeline (demonstrated by [LifeLines, Plaisant et al., 1996](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/257089.257391); [Health Timeline, Ledesma et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-019-0885-x); [HistoriView, Park et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2269-0995))
- Laboratory/symptom trend detection (demonstrated by [Morid et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.2196/14272))
- Clinical review alerts designed to minimize alert fatigue (informed by [Kwan et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m3216); [Ray et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag064); [Singh et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjmed.2009.07.027))

The reviewed literature does not show a single system that integrates all five elements. [EHR-RAG (Cao et al., 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21340) is the closest, combining RAG with longitudinal EHR data, but it focuses on structured EHR reasoning and does not include medical knowledge base QA, symptom/lab trend monitoring with predefined criteria, or clinical review alert generation. DocBot AI's defensible contribution is therefore the integration of approaches from separate research areas into a unified clinical information-support platform.

## 3. Research Gap Statement

Existing studies in medical AI primarily focus on individual capabilities: medical LLM/RAG systems address evidence-grounded question answering against curated knowledge bases or benchmarks ([Singhal et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06291-2); [Zakka et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2300068); [Sohn et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00300); [Zhang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.2196/84932)), longitudinal monitoring systems address temporal EHR modeling and patient timeline visualization ([Xie et al., 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1532046421003099); [Plaisant et al., 1996](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/257089.257391); [Ledesma et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-019-0885-x); [Park et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2269-0995)), and clinical decision-support systems address alert effectiveness and alert fatigue ([Kwan et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m3216); [Ray et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag064); [Singh et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjmed.2009.07.027)). While these approaches have been evaluated within their respective domains, few studies integrate evidence-grounded clinical QA with patient-specific longitudinal monitoring, trend detection, and clinical review alerts into a single platform. The closest system, [EHR-RAG (Cao et al., 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21340), combines RAG with longitudinal EHR data but focuses on structured EHR reasoning and does not incorporate medical knowledge base QA, symptom/lab trend monitoring with predefined criteria, or clinical review alert generation. The systematic review by [Basu & Huynh (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-026-14851-1) explicitly calls for integration of hallucination mitigation strategies into clinical workflows, and Med-PaLM/Med-PaLM 2 authors identify grounding in authoritative sources and real-world clinical workflow integration as needed research directions ([Singhal et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06291-2); [2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03423-7)).

A limitation identified across the reviewed literature is that evidence-grounded QA, longitudinal patient monitoring, trend detection, and clinical alerting are generally treated as separate research threads, with each component evaluated individually or in pairs but not as an integrated whole. [Kwan et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m3216) demonstrated that CDSS typically achieve only modest improvements (5.8%) with high heterogeneity, and explicitly called for "new ways of designing clinical decision support systems." [Singh et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjmed.2009.07.027) showed that simple threshold-based lab alerts are insufficient for ensuring timely follow-up. [Low et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1177/20552076251348850) found that no single LLM-based system is sufficient for all clinical question types. The literature therefore indicates an opportunity to design an integrated clinical information-support platform that combines evidence-grounded QA (using RAG and LLMs, as in [Almanac](https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2300068), [RAG²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00300), and [Zhang et al.](https://doi.org/10.2196/84932)), medical knowledge retrieval (as in [BioBERT](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btz682) and [SapBERT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11784)), longitudinal patient timeline organization (as in [LifeLines](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/257089.257391), [Health Timeline](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-019-0885-x), and [HistoriView](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2269-0995)), laboratory/symptom trend detection (as in [Morid et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.2196/14272)), and clinical review alerts informed by known alert-fatigue and follow-up limitations ([Kwan et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m3216); [Ray et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag064); [Singh et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjmed.2009.07.027)) — not as a claim of inventing any individual technique, but as a synthesis of approaches from separate research areas. DocBot AI addresses this opportunity by integrating these components into a single platform designed as a clinical information and review support system, not as an autonomous diagnostic or treatment decision-making tool.
